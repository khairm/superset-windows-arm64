**10. Job with `needs: [merge, build]` and `if: needs.merge.outputs.should_build == 'true'` (no status function), when `build` FAILS → job is SKIPPED. HIGH confidence.**

Two composing docs rules:
- "A default status check of `success()` is applied unless you include one of these functions" (`success`, `always`, `cancelled`, `failure`) — https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/workflows-and-actions/expressions
- "If a job fails or is skipped, all jobs that need it are skipped unless the jobs use a conditional expression" + "a failure or skip applies to all jobs in the dependency chain from the point of failure or skip onwards"; the page's only prescribed override is "use the `always()` conditional expression in `jobs.&lt;job_id&gt;.if`" — https://docs.github.com/en/actions/how-tos/write-workflows/choose-what-workflows-do/use-jobs

Empirical confirmation of the exact shape (multi-`needs` + outputs-only `if:`, upstream skipped → dependent skipped), with a GitHub-side reply calling it expected behavior and the accepted fix being `always() &amp;&amp; (&lt;expr&gt;)`: https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/26945

**Jobs vs steps:** same rule, different scope. The implicit-`success()` rule is not step-specific — the context-availability table allows `always, cancelled, success, failure` at both `jobs.&lt;job_id&gt;.if` and `jobs.&lt;job_id&gt;.steps.if`. Only the meaning differs: job level = all `needs` jobs succeeded; step level = all previous steps succeeded.

**Critical caveat flagged to them** (intersects finding #1 from the earlier batch): if `build` carries job-level `continue-on-error: true`, `needs.build.result` reports `success` despite the failure → implicit `success()` passes → the job RUNS on a red build. That is precisely the silent shipping bug. Hardening recommended: publish `outputs: ok: ${{ steps.b.outcome }}` from an `if: always()` step and assert `needs.build.outputs.ok == 'success'` explicitly — `outcome`, not `conclusion`.</result>
